About

I am an Assistant Professor of Operations and Information Systems at the David Eccles School of Business, University of Utah. My research sits at the intersection of technology, economics, and public policy, addressing fundamental questions about privacy, fairness, and responsibility in digital markets.

Using economic modeling and game-theoretic approaches, I examine how market structures, monetization strategies, organizational design, and ethical commitments shape outcomes in platform markets. I hold a Ph.D. in Management Science from the Joseph M. Katz Graduate School of Business, University of Pittsburgh.

Research

My research investigates how economic incentives and societal values shape the adoption and impact of technology. Early work analyzed dynamic pricing of new technology products and the economics of online advertising under competition and data regulation. Building on this, I have studied market-driven segmentation in equity crowdfunding and the broader question of how platform design shapes outcomes in digital markets.

My more recent work examines optimal governance structures in platform markets and the role of ethics in platform responsibility. I show that centralized coordination can outperform decentralized structures by harnessing information more effectively, and that ethically constrained platforms can outperform purely profit-driven platforms under bounded rationality—resolving moral hazard, reducing consumer risk, and initiating a virtuous cycle of higher effort, trust, and profitability.

Looking ahead, I extend these insights to AI-driven platforms and AI marketplaces, where transparency, alignment, and user control are central to ensuring that technological progress advances societal well-being.
